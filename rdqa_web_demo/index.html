The Challenge

Two Systems. One Truth.

Across Uganda, health programmes increasingly use digital platforms alongside the national paper-based HMIS. These parallel systems create data gaps that undermine programme credibility and donor confidence.

Digital Meets Paper

Digital health platforms capture real-time service delivery data via apps, SMS, and WhatsApp. Meanwhile, facility staff manually record the same services in HMIS registers (031, 055B, 071, 074, 080/081).

Discrepancies arise from transcription errors, staff capacity gaps, stock-outs of HMIS tools, misunderstanding of digital platforms, or even intentional data falsification. Our RDQA process bridges both systems with a rigorous three-way verification that no single-source audit can match.

Verification Factor (VF): VF = (Recounted / Reported) × 100%

Acceptable range: 90–110%. Below 90% = over-reporting. Above 110% = under-reporting.

Our Approach

Three Frameworks. One Methodology.

Our dual-system verification approach integrates three internationally recognised frameworks to ensure rigour, credibility, and alignment with Uganda's national standards.

MEASURE Evaluation RDQA Tool

Core structure for data verification (Part 1: recounting from source documents, computing Verification Factors) and systems assessment (Part 2: scoring M&E capacity across five functional areas).

WHO Data Quality Review Toolkit

Assessment across four quality dimensions: completeness & timeliness, internal consistency, external consistency, and population data comparisons.

Uganda MoH HMIS Guidelines

Alignment with national data quality attributes — accuracy, completeness, validity, consistency, uniqueness, and timeliness — using official HMIS registers and reporting tools.
